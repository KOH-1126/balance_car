According to the $\phi$ controller, if there is a $\phi$ which is negative, a forward acceleration, i.e. $\ddot{y} > 0$ ,will be produced to maintain $\ddot{\phi}=0$.
Therefore, speed could be controlled via controlling attitude.
![[Pasted image 20260907183131.png]]

e.g. I set $\dot{y}_{ref}$ as a positive number $v$. To form a negative $\phi$, wheels have to rotate inversely. If $\dot{y}$ is simply calculated by $\omega R$, which results $\dot{y}$ become smaller, $\dot{y}_{ref}-\dot{y}$ would become even larger due to wheels rotating inversely. That would ask $|\phi_{ref}|$ to become larger. Then wheels rotate more strongly in the inverse direction..... Eventually, a stable negative $\phi$ couldn't form. Instead, the car falls down!

In fact, if we only want to control the speed of the car body instead of the speed of wheels(i.e. define y as the position of the car body, not the position of the center of wheels. Meanwhile, $\dot{y}$ could be measured by MPU6050 fixed on the car body.), As a result, the positive feedback mentioned in the last paragraph would be turned into a negative feedback so that a stable speed can be produced. Detailed analysis is following:
![[无标题.png]]
From the geometrical relationship,
$$
\begin{align}
y & =p-L\sin\phi \\
\dot{y} & = \dot{p}-L\cos \phi \cdot \dot{\phi}
\end{align}
$$
From angular momentum theorem,
$$
\begin{align}
mL^2 \dot{\phi} & =(m \dot{p} \cos \phi+mg\sin \phi)L \\
\therefore L\dot{\phi} & = \dot{p}\cos \phi+g\sin \phi
\end{align}
$$
Combine two equations, we get:
$$
\dot{y} =(1-\cos^2 \phi) \dot{p}-g\sin \phi \cos \phi=2\sin^2 \frac{\phi}{2} \dot{p}-\frac{g}{2}\sin2\phi \tag{X}
$$

In [[倒立摆建模.pdf]], we know that, when $\phi$ is stable, i.e. $\ddot{\phi}=0$, $\ddot{y}=-g\tan \phi$.

Now just do some exaggerated assumption: $\ddot{y}=g$, so we have $\phi=-45^{\circ}$.
In this condition, let's check equation X, viewing $\dot{y}$ as a function of $\dot{p}$ :
![[屏幕截图 2026-09-08 022021.png]]

It shows that only when $\dot{p}$, which is generated by wheels rotating inversely, has an absolute larger than $16.73m/s^2$, will $\dot{y}$ become negative.
Actually for our balance car, $\ddot{y}$ must be much much smaller than $g$. Then the absolute of $\phi$ is much much smaller than 45°. In that case, the absolute of the zero of function X is much much larger than 16.73. Therefore, every time we command the car to move forward when $\dot{y}=0$, (i.e. set a positive $\dot{y}_{{ref}}$, then the system need to create a negative $\phi$, then wheels rotate inversely, which causes a negative $\dot{p}$) we are certain to get $\dot{y} > 0$. As a result, $\dot{y}_{ref}-y$ becomes smaller so that a negative feedback is formed, making a stable speed controller possible.
